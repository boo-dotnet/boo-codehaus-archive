## Description

Hello,

I was thinking about Boo's syntax, and here's an idea I had. The following are not ambiguous:

a

a:

a():

Fields, properties, and methods can all be distinguished without the need for def. So why require it?

Methods can be of the form mymethod():, properties of the form myprop:, and fields stay as they are.

This also adds more consistency between fields and the others.

It also lets you have a more natural operator overloading syntax. In C#, operator overloading uses

both static and operator, but all operators are static, so why require both? However, 'operator def' looks

unnatural, and 'def operator' makes one ask 'where is static?', since that usually goes before def.

But if def is optional, our syntax gets down to 'operator + (lhs, rhs):' which is very short and clean.

Let me know what you think.
